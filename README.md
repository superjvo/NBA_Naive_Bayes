# Predicting NBA Career Longevity Through Naive Bayes Classification 
Utilizing the Naive Bayes machine learning classification model to predict whether current NBA players will have a career longer than 5 years based on performance data. 
# Overview
The project goal in this scenario was to use machine learning techniques in order to predict NBA career duration as a binary classification variable (will/won't play 5 years). The data contained game and career stats for 1341 players in the NBA. Feature engineering was used to develop an improved list of variables that would provide greater predictive power and reduce redundancy. The dataset was the split into training and testing data to evaluate the model returning scores of 69% accuracy and 84% precision.
# Business Understanding
The high physical demand and constant influx of talent make the NBA a tough profession for sustained success. The average career length in this league is about 5 years with many players leaving in their first three. This high rate of turnover makes it imperative that team front offices and managers be very selective in talent acquisition to be more confident that a player they select will be able to contribute. Since the average NBA salary among the lower half of players is around 6-7 million organizations would be prudent to find players who will play long enough to provide a return on investment.
# Model Evaluation
The Gaussian Naive Bayes model used for classification returned scored of 69% accuracy and 84% recall. The high recall rate indicated the model is quite effective at classifying the majority of true positives being players whose career extended past 5 years. There was also a low number of false positives, however the false negative classifications were much higher. # Conclusion
The true objective the model will be used for will be the ultimate deciding factor in whether business decisions should be made by the front office staff. As it currently stands the accuracy of 69% isn't great and may benefit from additional data if available. If the ultimate goal is to correctly identify players who will contribute for an extended time frame I do believe this model is capable of highlighting talented players with a high potential to succeed. Since the model accurately predicts the number of true positives with a precision score of 84% coaches and scouts can be confident that the players in this category are unlikely to be busts. There will be a few false positives and a fair number of false negatives that come with using this model for roster decisions, but considering the financial cost of a player being a bust is much higher than passing on a player who may or may not have a long career; I am optimistic that this model can be utilized to improve a teams talent pool.
